01

Essence

Tagline
Honouring tradition. Creating the future.
Vision
To teach the world that fine wine is alcohol optional.
Mission
To be recognised as the highest quality non-alcoholic wine maker in the world.
Position
The Dom Pérignon of non-alcoholic wine.Premium $30–50 for the current range; Luxury $50+ for the Reserve collection.
The line that matters
We don't make alcohol-free wine. We make exceptional wine that happens to be non-alcoholic.

Origin

BOLLE is the non-alcoholic wine for discerning wine lovers who believe exceptional taste does not have to depend on alcohol. Through a pioneering secondary fermentation process, lees ageing and provenance, we capture the purest expression of wine. Wines that excel in appearance, aroma, flavour and texture, exactly as they should.

What makes us different

  • Twice fermented. Unlike the rest of the category, BOLLE ferments a second time, including after dealcoholisation. This is the single most important thing to communicate.
  • Lees aged. Extended contact with the lees builds the texture and complexity that non-alcoholic wine is usually missing.
  • Provenance and IP. Grapes from distinguished regions, and a substantial body of intellectual property behind the process.
  • Product philosophy. Each bottle carries a narrative of passion, dedication and respect for nature, crafted by generations of expertise.

Values

Language rules

Most-referenced section

Always

  • Write BOLLE in all caps
  • Use non-alcoholic, hyphenated
  • Keep the accent on Rosé
  • Write Blanc de Blancs with the second s. It is silent when spoken
  • Spell it Silvaner, never Sylvaner
  • Write ABV as <0.5% ABV or "less than 0.5% ABV"
  • Use US spelling for US content
  • Include neat links to product pages on social posts
  • Use the correct product images and taglines at every touchpoint

Never

  • Say alcohol-free. Non-alcoholic instead
  • Use bright or neon colours in design or graphics
  • Place busy backgrounds or patterns behind the logo or product images
  • Alter the logo's colour or proportions
  • Use the wrong glassware in visuals
  • Use emojis or excessive exclamation marks
  • Use GIFs in stories
  • Use #887711. Retired, see section 05

07

Logo

Primary, on dark
Primary, on light
Roundel
Bubble icon
  • Primary logo is the wordmark, with the O rendered as a ring and two bubbles rising from it. Use it wherever there is room.
  • Roundel works as an accent or decorative element, especially on merchandise and digital assets.
  • Bubble icon. The O with its bubbles, standing alone. Use it sparingly, as an icon or watermark.
  • Clear space: keep space around the logo equal to the height of the letter B in BOLLE. Nothing intrudes on it.
  • Never alter the colour or the proportions, and never place a busy background or pattern behind it.

The ring-and-rising-bubbles gesture is the brand's one distinctive graphic idea. It is the correct starting point for any texture, pattern or graphic device, see section 12.

08

Imagery

  • Style: sophisticated, elegant, modern.
  • Setups: minimal, with clean natural lighting. The bottle and the glass are the subject.
  • Glassware matters. Use the correct glass for the wine. The wrong glassware undermines the entire premium claim.
  • No busy backgrounds behind product images, and no pattern competing with the label.
  • No bright or neon colour anywhere in the frame or in graphics laid over it.

Audience

PriorityWhoWhat convinces them
P1: Trade Sommeliers, wine directors, owners of premium and luxury on-trade. Michelin-starred restaurants, 5 AA Rosettes, 5-star and 5 AA Red Star hotels, James Beard winners, luxury travel. The wine, tasted blind. Provenance, technique, credibility.
P1: Consumer Sophisticated wine enthusiasts, 30–65+, with developed palates. Moderating for many reasons. Pregnancy, medication, health, driving, early commitments, or simply because they prefer it. Looking and reading like fine wine. Understanding what makes it different.
P2 Mid-premium on-trade: Michelin listed, 3 & 4 AA Rosettes, 4-star hotels, Pinnacle Guide bars, premium travel, luxury catering and events, Nielsen Platinum venues. Luxury off-trade: prestigious premium independents and wine merchants. Category leadership and shelf presence.
P3 Gold on-trade: 1 & 2 AA Rosettes, 3 AA Red Star hotels, Nielsen Gold venues. Mid-premium off-trade: premium chains. Recognition and rate of sale.

Consumer mindsets

  • Discerning quality seekers. Value authenticity and craftsmanship
  • Inclusive hosts. Believe every guest should feel welcomed and celebrated
  • Health-conscious luxury consumers. Want premium experiences without compromise
  • Progressive wine lovers. Open to innovation while respecting tradition
  • Celebration curators. Seek to create meaningful shared moments

The standing tension. Revenue is trade-led, and the trade is convinced by tasting. Consumers cannot taste first, so look and explanation do all the work.

As retail distribution widens, consumer work must avoid discount language, mass-market cues and "now available everywhere". Frame wider availability as wider access to something still exceptional. Premium on-trade accounts are watching.

How to update

  • Colour and type changes start on the site. The live storefronts are the source of truth, and this guide follows them.
  • Log every change in the version history above, with the reason. A guide without a changelog stops being trusted.
  • Bump the version on the cover and reissue. Anyone holding an older copy should be able to tell.
  • Owner is responsible for approving changes and for retiring superseded copies.
